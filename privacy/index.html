Trieu AR Glasses privacy policy

Last updated 8 November 2026, for version 0.33.0 of the Android app.

Trieu AR Glasses is a phone app that works with Everysight Maverick glasses. It translates what you see and hear, helps you find your way, and shows things like the time and your steps on the glasses. This page says what the app reads, what it keeps, what it sends, and to whom.

In short
  • There is no account and no sign-in. The app's maker runs no server and receives none of your data.
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking, and nothing is sold.
  • Translating text and speech happens on your phone.
  • What the app keeps (pictures, clips, a diary, settings, your keys) stays on your phone, in the app's own storage, and is left out of phone backups.
  • Some features send things to outside services: your position to map, weather and review services; your question, and a picture if you asked about one, to the AI assistant you chose. Each is listed below.
  • Health data is read only, kept only in memory, and never sent anywhere.

Who we are

The app is made by Trieu. For any question about this policy or your data, email jtplanet@gmail.com.

What stays on your phone

  • Speech and text recognition and translation. Speech from the glasses' microphone and text from the glasses' camera are recognised and translated on the phone, with models you download once. The recordings and pictures used for this are not kept or sent.
  • Pictures and video you take. "Yo, pic", "Yo, record" and security mode save pictures, video and sound to the app's own storage, with a note of where you were and what was being read or heard. Security mode records video and sound continuously until you turn it off, so it can record other people: please follow the local rules on recording.
  • The diary and the places log. A line with the time and place for each picture, translation, place and plan of the day.
  • Settings, the API keys you paste in, your home base and your travel-group code.
  • Downloaded maps and places for a city you chose to keep offline.
  • Movie mode. The subtitles the camera reads off your TV, and the film's dialogue the microphone hears, are read and translated on the phone, said aloud and forgotten. To notice the film pausing it compares each camera picture, as a coarse grid of brightness, with the one before, on the phone; the grid is forgotten too. Nothing is kept or sent.

All of this is in the app's own storage. You can see, share, export or delete it under Saved items in the app. It is deleted when you uninstall the app, and it is left out of cloud backups and phone-to-phone transfers on purpose, because it includes your keys and positions.

Health data

Health data is off until you turn on Use health data in the app's Health tab, and then Android's Health Connect asks you which kinds the app may read.

  • What is read: today's steps, your latest heart rate and today's resting heart rate, and the calories burned today. Only the kinds you allow.
  • What it is used for: showing these numbers on the phone's Health tab and, if you leave the health card on, on the glasses' display. Nothing else.
  • Kept: no. Each reading stays in the phone's memory until the next one and is gone when the app closes. Nothing is written to Health Connect.
  • Sent: never. Not to the AI assistants, not to the app's maker, not to anyone. Health data is not used for advertising, profiling or any purpose other than showing it to you.
  • Reading in the background, if you allow it, only lets the card keep up while the phone is locked.
  • Take it back at any time in Health Connect, or by turning Use health data off.

The app's use of information received from Health Connect follows the Google Play's health permissions policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

What is sent, and to whom

Every service below is contacted directly from your phone. Like any website, each one sees your phone's internet address when it is contacted. Each has its own privacy policy.

Your position

  • Wikipedia (*.wikipedia.org: the Wikipedia in your phone's language, and en.wikipedia.org) and the OpenStreetMap search servers (overpass-api.de, overpass.openstreetmap.fr, overpass.kumi.systems): where you are, to find what is around you.
  • Nominatim (nominatim.openstreetmap.org): your position rounded to about a kilometre, to know which country you are in.
  • Valhalla (valhalla1.openstreetmap.de): where you are and where you are walking to, for directions.
  • Open-Meteo (api.open-meteo.com): your position rounded to about a kilometre, for the weather.
  • US National Weather Service (api.weather.gov): where you are, for weather warnings, only in the United States.
  • Yelp (api.yelp.com) and Tripadvisor (terra.tripadvisor.com): where you are, for ratings of places nearby, only if you add a key for them.
  • Your travel group, only if you turn on Share my position: your name, position and day plan go every two minutes through a relay (ntfy.sh, or one you set up). They are encrypted with a key made from your group's code, so the relay cannot read them.

Your questions to an AI assistant

Questions the app cannot answer by itself go to the assistant you chose, using the key you pasted in: Anthropic Claude (api.anthropic.com), Google Gemini (generativelanguage.googleapis.com) or OpenAI ChatGPT (api.openai.com). The assistant gets the text of your question. For "what am I looking at" it also gets a picture from the glasses' camera, and for questions about what is near you, your position. If you turn on Identify what I look at by itself (off unless you turn it on), a picture goes to the assistant each time your eyes rest on something for two seconds. The assistant may search the web to answer. Nothing is sent to an assistant until you add its key. Your requests are covered by the policy of the company whose key you use.

Things that do not include your position

  • News: the headlines you chose are read from feeds.bbci.co.uk, news.google.com, feeds.npr.org, www.theguardian.com, www.aljazeera.com, rss.dw.com and any feed you add yourself.
  • Weather warnings: feeds.meteoalarm.org, data.weather.gov.hk and www.gdacs.org. The phone reads their whole feeds and picks out what concerns you.
  • Stocks: the symbols you follow, to Finnhub (finnhub.io), only if you add a key.
  • Currency: the two currencies you are converting between, to Frankfurter (api.frankfurter.dev).
  • Downloads: speech models from alphacephei.com, and translation and text-recognition models from Google through ML Kit. Google's ML Kit runs on the phone; Google says it may collect anonymous diagnostic and usage information about the library itself, not the text or pictures it reads.

Pages and apps the app opens for you

Some buttons open another app or a web page on your phone, which then works under its own policy: a map on www.openstreetmap.org; Yelp and Tripadvisor pages (www.yelp.com, www.tripadvisor.com) to read or write a review, with your review text on the clipboard for you to paste; a ride in Uber (m.uber.com) or Lyft (lyft.com); Health Connect on the Play Store (play.google.com); and your messaging app, with the picture attached, for you to send yourself. The app never posts, sends or books anything without you tapping it in that app.

The glasses

The app talks to the glasses over Bluetooth, using Everysight's software library. Everysight's own apps and services, if you use them, are covered by Everysight's privacy policy.

Children

The app is not meant for children under 13 and does not knowingly collect anything from them.

Your choices

  • Turn off location, contacts, health data or any other permission in Android's settings or in Health Connect; the features that need it stop, and the rest keep working.
  • Leave out a key, and the service it is for is never contacted.
  • Delete pictures, clips and the diary under Saved items, or everything by uninstalling the app.
  • Because the maker holds none of your data, there is nothing to ask us to delete. For data a service above received, ask that service.

Changes to this policy

This page changes whenever a new version of the app reads, keeps or sends something new. The date at the top says when it last changed.
